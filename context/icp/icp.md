# PaidHR: Ideal Customer Profile (v2)

Who PaidHR sells to: firmographics, current tools, intent signals, buyer roles, readiness and non-fits. Read before any targeting, list building or copy.
v3, 2026-11-12. Geography widened from Nigeria to Nigeria, Kenya and Ghana (user decision 2026-11-12). v2 rebuilt from `context/docs/research/` only and replaced v1 (2026-11-11); v1 hypotheses with no research support moved to [Open questions](#7-open-questions).
Related: [personas.md](personas.md), [value-proposition.md](../positioning/value-proposition.md), [competitors.md](../positioning/competitors.md).

## Summary

A company in **Nigeria, Kenya or Ghana** with **20 to 500 employees (core 50 to 500)** that pays staff locally, must remit statutory deductions every month (in Nigeria: PAYE, pension, NHF and NSITF), and still runs payroll on spreadsheets, a manual remittance process or a provider it no longer trusts.

- **Scope decisions (agreed with user 2026-11-11 and 2026-11-12, see [work-log](../../work-log/2026-W46.md))**: Nigeria, Kenya and Ghana (Kenya and Ghana added 2026-11-12). Tier 1 size band 50 to 500. Vuzo / embedded partners excluded.
- **Evidence by country**: every customer, quote and statutory claim in the research is Nigerian. Kenya is named as a market in funding coverage (co:S4, S5); Ghana is named only in a CEO quote and a PaidHR blog table (kg:KG2, KG3). Until open question 10 is answered, Nigeria is the proven market and Kenya and Ghana are expansion markets: list building may include them, but copy sent there may not use Nigerian statutory or customer proof.

## 1. Firmographic

- **Country: proven**: Nigeria. Product is built on Nigerian PAYE, pension, NHF, NSITF. Source: co:S1; scope decision.
- **Country: expansion**: Kenya and Ghana. Kenya named as a PaidHR market in funding coverage (co:S4, S5); CEO says PaidHR already supports companies in Kenya, South Africa and Ghana (kg:KG2); PaidHR blog lists Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana as its regional focus (kg:KG3). Local statutory support in either country: unknown (open question 10). Local competitors: Workpay (Nairobi HQ), SeamlessHR (offices in Nairobi and Accra), Oze (Ghana). Source: scope decision 2026-11-12; cp:K1, K6, K7.
- **Size: plan floor**: 20 employees (Standard plan "20 - 100"; Enterprise "100+"). Source: co:S6.
- **Size: investor ICP**: "Under 200 employees". Source: cu:C4.
- **Size: current average**: ~62 to ~75 employees per customer (derived from PaidHR totals). Source: cu: Company sizes.
- **Size: on-camera customers**: Cleva "almost 20"; Go54 "between 50 and 70". Source: sp:V7, sp:V6.
- **Size: ceiling evidence**: First customer TSL had 2,000 employees; LinkedIn targets "fast-growing and enterprise organizations". Source: cu:C1, co:S2.
- **Industries named by PaidHR / investors**: Logistics, retail, manufacturing; tech startups. Source: cu:C3, cu:C4.
- **Industries of customers on camera**: Fintech (Mono, Cleva, Rise), insurtech (MyCoverGenius), web hosting (Go54), cinema (Filmhouse), health tech (FAMASI), solar engineering (Sygnite). Source: sp:V1 to V10.
- **Tier 2 add-on**: Staff or contractors paid in other currencies (Cross-Border Payroll, 45+ / 49 currencies). Source: co:S1, co:S8.
- **Note**: PaidHR names logistics, retail and manufacturing as its main sectors (cu:C3), but every customer on camera with a stated size is office-based or service-based. See open question 1.

## 2. Technographic (what they use today)

- **Excel / spreadsheets for payroll**: "Gigantic Excel spreadsheet" (Cleva); "the Excel sheet" (Go54); "multiple spreadsheets" (PaidHR copy). Source: sp:V7, sp:V6, posts.
- **Manual or outsourced statutory remittance**: Accounting firm with someone "literally walking to go to the tax office" (Cleva); manual remittance and confirmation (FAMASI). Source: sp:V7, sp:V1.
- **Another payroll provider that is failing or slow**: Filmhouse's previous administrator "had internal issues"; Rise's previous platform paid pension "three to four months" late. Source: sp:V5, sp:V8.
- **Ex-Bento Africa customers**: Bento collapsed Feb 2025 after tax and pension remittance probes; some clients' staff unpaid. Source: cp:K9.
- **Global tools for foreign staff**: Deel already serves African businesses; Cadana for remote-first teams. Source: cp:K5, cp:K10, cp:K11.
- **Foreign software not fitted to Nigeria**: Xceed365HR CEO: many Nigerian firms run payroll on foreign software. Source: cp:K11.
- **Free local tools (small end)**: NotchHR (free HR), Salario (free payroll). Source: cp:K11.

## 3. Intent signals

Only signals that appear in the research. Detection methods are open question 6.

- **Team growth breaks the spreadsheet**: "As we grew it just didn't scale" (Cleva); "What used to work for a smaller team, it can no longer work for us" (MyCoverGenius); "You notice them when they start slowing everyone down" (PaidHR copy). Source: sp:V7, sp:V3, posts.
- **Contractors moving to full-time staff**: Cleva: moving to full-time brought PAYE and state-by-state remittance. Source: sp:V7.
- **Current payroll provider fails**: Filmhouse switched after provider's "internal issues"; Bento collapse. Source: sp:V5, cp:K9.
- **Remittance errors or delays**: Go54: payments that "may not reflect at the statutory bodies"; Rise: pension months late. Source: sp:V6, sp:V8.
- **Compliance pressure from leadership**: Mono: compliance "a huge bone of contention". Source: sp:V4.
- **New year onboarding wave**: Sygnite: "A new year means new team members joining". Source: sp:V10.
- **Nigerian tax law changes effective 1 Jan 2026**: Third-party listing notes PaidHR supports them. Source: co:S13.
- **CEO asks HR to look at a tool**: Go54: "my CEO asked me to look into [PaidHR]". Source: sp:V6.
- **Wants to offer pension**: Cleva: founders "always wanted to do pension". Source: sp:V7.

## 4. Persona

Full detail in [personas.md](personas.md).

- **Champion**: HR lead: Head of HR, Head of People, HR Operations Lead. Evidence: Go54, Mono, MyCoverGenius, Filmhouse, Real Fruit. Source: sp:V3 to V6, V9.
- **Economic buyer**: CEO / founder (payroll owner in small teams; initiator in larger ones). Evidence: FAMASI, Cleva, Go54 CEO. Source: sp:V1, V7, V6.
- **Influencer**: Finance lead / CFO. Evidence: Go54 CFO did separate payroll checks. Source: sp:V6.
- **End users**: Employees (app, PaidLife, WhatsApp, USSD). Evidence: Rise employee. Source: sp:V8; posts.

## 5. Engagement readiness

A lead is ready when it shows the signals in section 3 **and** these conditions hold:

- **Ready when a named payroll owner exists (HR lead, or founder in small teams)**: Every on-camera buyer owned payroll personally. Source: sp:V1 to V10.
- **Ready when they will take a demo**: Demo was the decision moment for Go54, Filmhouse, Cleva. Source: sp:V5, V6, V7.
- **Ready when they can start fast**: No upfront onboarding fee; sign up, onboard and run payroll same day. Source: co:S7.

Channels that have worked:

- **Founder-led social**: Cleva found PaidHR through its CEO's Twitter posts and sent a DM. Source: sp:V7.
- **CEO-to-HR referral**: Go54 CEO told Head of HR to look at PaidHR. Source: sp:V6.
- **Customer referral**: FAMASI CEO: "I have been recommending already". Source: sp:V1.
- **"Book a demo" CTA**: On most PaidHR LinkedIn posts (bit.ly/PaidHR_Rqd). Source: posts.
- **Webinars and events**: Thrive Series webinars; GITEX Nigeria 2026; Moonshot 2026. Source: posts.

## 6. Not a fit

- **Under 20 employees**: Below Standard plan floor; free tools target this band. Source: co:S6, cp:K11.
- **No payroll in Nigeria, Kenya or Ghana**: Staff paid only outside the three target countries. Statutory remittance, the core module, is evidenced for Nigeria only. Source: co:S1; scope decision 2026-11-12.
- **Needs Africa-wide EOR or 54-country payroll**: Workpay owns that position; PaidHR has no evidence of non-Nigerian entities. Source: cp:K7, co: Gaps.
- **HR-tech, payroll or EOR vendors**: Competitors or partners, not customers. Source: scope decision.
- **Over 2,000 employees**: No evidence of fit above first customer TSL; SeamlessHR serves enterprise with SAP/Oracle integrations. Source: cu:C1, cp:K1, cp:K2.

## 7. Open questions

1. Are logistics, retail and manufacturing really the core, when all on-camera customers are office-based? Why it matters: decides Tier 1 industry and copy.
2. Which customers retain and expand best (size, industry)? Why it matters: confirms the 50 to 500 band.
3. ACV and price per employee. Why it matters: sets lead value; pricing is not public (co:S6).
4. Who signs at 50 to 100 vs 200 to 500 employees? Why it matters: confirms buyer roles.
5. Win/loss vs SeamlessHR, Workpay, free tools. Why it matters: no data in research (cp: Gaps).
6. Which external signals (job posts, funding, headcount) predict a buy? Why it matters: v1 signals were hypotheses; not in research.
7. Are public sector, informal businesses and parent-mandated HRIS firms poor fits? Why it matters: v1 listed them; no research support.
8. Does PaidLife or WhatsApp/USSD influence the buying decision, or only adoption? Why it matters: lead message for frontline firms.
9. Customer count: 400+ or 570+? Why it matters: both on homepage (co:S1).
10. Kenya and Ghana: can PaidHR run local payroll there (not only cross-border pay), which statutory deductions does it compute and remit in each, and is there a customer it can name? Why it matters: until answered, no statutory or customer claim may go to Kenyan or Ghanaian prospects, and W3 holds sends there ([W3 spec](../../output/campaigns/new-hr-leader-first-100-days/workflow-spec.md) open item 5). **Public check 2026-11-12 ([kenya-ghana-check.md](../docs/research/kenya-ghana-check.md))**: (a) PARTIAL: CEO says PaidHR supports companies in Kenya, South Africa and Ghana, Accion says 250+ businesses across Nigeria, Kenya and South Africa, but growth is framed as cross-border payroll; (b) Kenya PARTIAL (PaidHR blog claims Kenyan statutory deductions, no deduction named anywhere), Ghana no evidence; (c) no Kenyan or Ghanaian customer named. Still needs PaidHR to confirm.
